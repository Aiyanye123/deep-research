# Research Depth

Choose effort from the question's breadth, difficulty, available material, and
consequences. A narrow question may have a few decisive sources; a broad synthesis
may require extensive reading across fields or contexts. Long-form work needs
enough substance to develop the requested length without repetition.

There is no default quota for sources, queries, domains, textual anchors, waves,
or counterarguments. Do not inflate activity counts or use them as proof of depth.

## What Makes Further Work Worthwhile

- A consequential fact or conclusion remains uncertain and can be checked.
- Another perspective or source type could explain an important contradiction.
- The available material only represents one interested party or one framing.
- A promising comparison or connection could materially deepen understanding.
- An important quotation, measurement, definition, or inference needs verification.

Evaluate source quality, relevance, independence, context, and what it actually
establishes. Read consequential material. Search snippets and repeated reporting
of one origin cannot substitute for it. For literary work, primary-text attention
matters more than the number of external references.

## When To Stop

Stop when the central inquiry has a supported answer and additional work is
unlikely to improve it materially. If a tractable unknown could still change the
conclusion, pursue it. If evidence is unavailable, acknowledge the limit and
adjust the conclusion rather than pretending the question is settled.

This judgment needs no saturation certificate or mandatory final search waves.
Optional managed-runtime budgets and numerical policies remain defined by that
runtime; they do not govern the default research workflow.
