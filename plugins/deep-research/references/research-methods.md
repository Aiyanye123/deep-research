# Research Methods by Question Type

Choose methods that illuminate the question. Mix approaches when another field
or kind of evidence adds understanding, without a compulsory method label or
written justification. The options below are guidance, not a checklist.

User files, pasted text, datasets, and drafts can supply all or part of the
material. Read them with tools suited to their format. They may be primary works,
records, research papers, creative material, or text to revise. Add web sources
when the task calls for current information, wider context, or independent
verification and the user permits it. File-only writing needs no external-source
quota. Distinguish what the material states from what the article infers.

## Literary And Cultural Interpretation

Begin with the work itself: scenes, passages, form, sequence, voice, images, omissions, performance, and reception where relevant. Keep useful passage locations when needed and distinguish observation from interpretation. Details may support several readings or remain unresolved; preserve that ambiguity when the work permits it. External criticism can supply context and competing readings when useful, but is not a prerequisite or substitute for close reading. A useful result can refine a familiar interpretation, show where it applies, or explain why the text does not settle a question.

## Factual Investigation

Frame the question around what happened, who did what, when, and how confidently it can be established. Build a timeline or entity map when needed. Prefer records close to the event, identify how each source obtained its information, and corroborate consequential claims through independent evidence where possible. Separate confirmed facts, attributed accounts, inference, allegation, and unresolved detail. If the record cannot establish an answer, describe the limit and what evidence would be needed.

## Academic Review

Define the field, research question, inclusion boundary, and review method at a scale that fits the brief. Map major concepts, schools, methods, evidence bases, and disagreements. Compare what studies actually measure and support; do not treat citation frequency as proof or imply systematic coverage without a systematic method. A review may establish consensus, identify a bounded disagreement, synthesize compatible findings, or state that the literature cannot yet answer the question.

## Market And Company Analysis

Define the market, geography, period, customer or unit, and metric before comparing claims. Separate company statements from filings, product evidence, regulator data, independent reporting, competitor material, and customer signals. Check denominators, accounting definitions, incentives, and time windows. Treat forecasts and scenarios as conditional. State when available data supports only a directional assessment or cannot support a market-size or causal claim.

## Technical Research

Anchor the work to the relevant product, version, environment, standard, and use case. Prefer specifications, official documentation, source code, standards, issue records, and reproducible measurements as appropriate. Separate documented behavior from observed behavior, inference, and recommendation. For implementation or performance claims, state test conditions and limitations; do not generalize beyond them. Explain unresolved compatibility or operational questions directly.

## Mixed Briefs

Select the primary method by asking which kind of evidence must carry the central answer. Add secondary methods for distinct subquestions: for example, a company investigation may need technical verification of a product claim, or literary criticism may need factual research into production context. Keep the evidence standards and uncertainty labels of each method visible in the final synthesis.
