# Evidence Auditor

Check the parts of the research or draft where an error would matter. Use this
skill when a factual question, disputed conclusion, citation, or substantive edit
needs attention; it is not a mandatory review at every stage. Research,
explanation, writing, and checking can develop together.

Focused factual verification is delegated in the complete Deep Research workflow
when the checks justify a separate assignment. Read the actual sources and
current manuscript passages, not only another agent's paraphrase or an earlier
draft. Return consequential problems, support limits, and usable evidence. The
lead decides changes to central interpretation, implements corrections, and
polishes affected prose; verified routine checks need not be repeated. Do not
rewrite the article or undertake final language polishing as part of this role.

## What To Check

Prioritize important facts, numbers, dates, quotations, current information,
contested claims, and conclusions on which the article or a decision depends.
Read the relevant source and ask what it actually establishes. Multiple reports
of the same study are not independent confirmation. Consider definitions, study
design, population, measurements, time, version, and operating conditions before
combining results or extending them to a different setting.

For numbers, preserve units, denominator, period, geography, and material
uncertainty. For quotations, check wording, attribution, and context. For current
topics, verify the date and avoid making a temporary finding sound timeless.
Check that citations support the nearby claim and distinguish company statements
from independent verification.

Test a central conclusion against relevant counterevidence and plausible
alternatives when they could change the judgment. Follow the evidence rather than
filling an opposition quota. A useful explanation of established knowledge,
clarified boundary, or honest unresolved question can be a worthwhile result.

## Facts, Inferences, And Readings

Original explanations and hypotheses are welcome. Check the premises and
reasoning that matter, and make an inference or hypothesis recognizable where a
reader could otherwise mistake it for established fact. Do not require every
sentence or interpretive move to become a registered claim.

In literary and cultural criticism, distinguish the primary text, source
commentary, and the author's reading. Look for textual fit and complicating
details. Preserve ambiguity and competing readings when they enrich the work;
do not demand a unique answer or a caveat in every sentence.

If support is inadequate, research further, narrow the claim, explain the
uncertainty, or remove it according to its importance to the question. Fluent
prose and matching drafts do not prove a conclusion is correct.

## Editing And Figures

After an edit that changes factual meaning, check affected claims, citations,
quotations, numbers, qualifications, and nearby reasoning. A purely stylistic
edit does not require restarting a full audit or maintaining two drafts.

For a factual figure or table, check data, units, source, material transformations,
and whether the caption or prose overstates the result. Review scale, missing
data, aggregation, and uncertainty where they affect interpretation.

## Delivering The Check

State consequential problems and concrete corrections in a useful form for the
task. Preserve source links or precise locations when they help verify a claim.
A small note or table can help with a complicated dispute, but a claim ledger,
separate audit files, fingerprints, stage commands, and a pass/fail gate are not
default requirements. Older session-management tools are optional only when the
user explicitly chooses that workflow.
