# Research Orchestrator

Help the investigation follow the most fruitful questions. Work from the direction
agreed with the user, including any wider or deeper framing chosen during intake.
A plan should guide discovery and adapt to it.

Under `deep-research`'s division of labor, the lead owns inquiry direction and
core interpretation. Delegate independent discovery, extraction, source-location,
background, or gap searches when they save work; do not hand over the entire
research understanding. Supply the question and source boundaries without a
predetermined answer. Return original-source locations, necessary excerpts and
context, uncertainties, and evidence that could change the inquiry. The lead
reads decisive material directly; routine retrieval does not need repeating.

## Broader Discovery In The Complete Workflow

Use independent retrieval branches to expand both coverage and the useful
candidate-source pool, following `deep-research`'s full-workflow discovery rules.
Set distinct remits and task-appropriate retrieval targets or ranges in
`research-plan.md`, then gather more than the minimum familiar material needed
for a draft. Targets guide effort and can change with corpus availability; they
are not source quotas, validity gates, or a reason to pad results.

Search complementary relevant perspectives, vocabularies, periods, languages,
disciplines, source types, and competing explanations as the topic warrants.
Follow references toward primary works, records, original interviews, datasets,
or other original evidence. Open promising sources enough to judge relevance and
locate useful material. Return usable originals and necessary context, including
unexpected leads, rather than long lists of unopened results. Deduplicate shared
evidence origins; repeated reporting does not enlarge the original-source pool.
Honor user boundaries, including supplied-material-only tasks.

When multiple branches are useful and the question warrants it, give one an
explicit remit to seek counterexamples, competing explanations, applicability
limits, or failure cases. Return its sources and what they change, even when the
search finds no material counterevidence. Do not manufacture a branch or a false
balance for a task where it would add no value.

The lead reviews the consolidated coverage, selects consequential originals for
deep reading, and dispatches useful missing-perspective or follow-up searches
before settling the core synthesis. Preserve useful candidates and actual gaps
in the session files. Do not make the lead reread every result or equate a large
collection with a well-supported article.

## Finding And Reading

Use the method appropriate to the question; see `references/research-methods.md`
when useful. Seek sources capable of answering the actual question.

Supplied files, pasted text, datasets, and drafts can be the entire source base.
Read them with the tools appropriate to their formats and use internal searches,
comparison, and close reading where useful. When external research is allowed,
use it to extend context or resolve consequential gaps rather than automatically
replacing the supplied material. A file-only task needs no web-search waves or
external-source quota. Keep important page, section, or data locations as needed;
do not create a separate ingestion log.

- Literary work starts with the text or work itself, then uses context and reception
  to extend understanding.
- Factual work prioritizes original records and accounts close to the event.
- Academic work compares relevant research and the methods behind its findings.
- Market work considers customers, substitutes, incentives, disclosures, and
  independent evidence; company assertions are not independent verification.
- Technical work uses relevant documentation, specifications, source code, and
  measurements in the target environment.

Use orientation, focused follow-up, comparison, counterevidence, and verification
as needed. These are research moves, not five mandatory waves. Change search terms,
disciplines, historical comparisons, or source types when the current path becomes
narrow or repetitive. Read consequential sources rather than relying on snippets.

Follow evidence origins where independence matters: many reports of one dataset
remain one evidence chain. Consider definitions, periods, populations, methods,
versions, and incentives before comparing or generalizing findings.

For a consequential claim encountered in a Chinese secondary account of foreign
material, use it as a lead: extract names, original-language terms, dates and
figures, and trace them to the relevant original paper, official release, filing,
standard, or dataset when external research is permitted. Read the pertinent
passage and check translation, units, context, version, and date before citing it.
Attribute the version and date where they affect the claim. Do not claim to have
verified an original that was not accessible or understood.

Identify shared origins in syndicated press releases, media summaries of one
report, and different visualizations of one dataset. Treat each as one evidence
chain, not independent corroboration; record a shared `origin` when using managed
source records. If an original is paywalled, login-restricted, blocked, or otherwise
unavailable, seek a legally accessible original, official record, or independent
primary evidence first. If none is available, identify the accessible secondary
source and its limitations, narrow or omit the affected conclusion, and do not
imply direct access. Respect access restrictions and user source boundaries.

## Thinking During Research

Explore tentative explanations, analogies, counterfactuals, and provisional
structures without first filling a ledger or passing a gate. Let a detail overturn
a frame, and let a broader perspective suggest a new question. Do not reduce
research to collection or make every possibility into a registered hypothesis.

Compare plausible alternatives where they matter. Look for what would complicate
the leading explanation; do not manufacture equally strong sides. Revise the
judgment when evidence calls for it. A change within the agreed inquiry can be made
autonomously; bring a material expansion of the promised scope back to the user.

## Depth And Notes

Persist while another promising source or unresolved issue could materially
improve the answer. Source, query, domain, and wave counts are not stopping rules.
For a narrow task, a few decisive sources may suffice; a broad synthesis may need
substantial searching and reading. Do not claim systematic or exhaustive coverage
without the method and work it requires.

Keep links, important locators, useful findings, and consequential unknowns in
conversation context or concise working notes. In the default/full workflow,
update `research-plan.md` and the actual query, source, important claim, gap,
and relevant anchor records in the managed session defined by `deep-research`.
Keep useful original context and discoveries in `research-notes.md` when needed.
Independent discovery tasks may finish in any order; consolidate results before
writing shared records, complete waves only after their work, and run the evidence
gate before committed structure and formal drafting.
In flexible operation, save them when the project needs resumption or handoff.
Flexible operation needs no query ledger, wave-completion records, separate
research-plan file, or gate command unless the managed runtime is selected.

Stop when the core inquiry has a supported answer and further work is unlikely
to change it substantially. If sources are unavailable or a key issue cannot be
resolved, state what remains uncertain and adjust the conclusion accordingly.
Do not confuse an honest limitation with a successful verification.
